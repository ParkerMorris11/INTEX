# Sources

The case is explicit: *"If including statistics, you should be able to explain
where your data and information sources come from"* and *"Claim AI-generated
content as your original research without attribution"* is listed under what you
may **not** do.

Every figure in the tool links to its source at the point of use. This file is
the consolidated list, plus the caveats you should be able to state out loud.

**Retrieved: 3 September 2026.** Salary figures change — re-check before relying
on any number in an actual negotiation.

---

## 1. BYU placement and starting salary

**Source:** BYU IS Careers — Placement Stats
<https://iscareers.byu.edu/placement-stats>

| Class | BSIS placement | BSIS avg salary | MISM placement | MISM avg salary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 93% | $73,130 | 93% | $93,865 |
| 2024 | 75% | $76,211 | 94% | $89,797 |
| 2025 | 91% | $72,487 | 90% | $91,826 |

**What to notice, and be ready to explain:**

- **The 2024 BSIS placement dip to 75%** is real and visible in the data. If an
  evaluator asks, do not talk around it — it reflects the 2024 tech hiring
  contraction, and it recovered to 91% in 2025.
- **BSIS starting salary is roughly flat-to-down across three years** ($73,130 →
  $76,211 → $72,487) while national medians for these occupations are well over
  $100,000. That is not a contradiction; it is the difference between a starting
  salary and an all-experience median. This is the core teaching point of Module 1.
- These are **averages, not medians**, and they cover the whole BSIS cohort
  rather than any single career path. We do not break them down by role because
  the department does not publish that breakdown, and inventing one would be
  exactly the kind of unattributed claim the case prohibits.

**Caveats you should be able to state:**

- A BLS median is **all experience levels pooled**. It is not an entry-level
  number and should never be quoted as one.
- Software Developer and QA Tester share one BLS occupation group, which is why
  their figures are close.
- The IT Project Manager figure is the broader "project management specialists"
  category, not IT-specific. Robert Half lists IT project managers specifically
  at $103,500–$147,000.

survey. Ranges from different guides for the same role can differ by $50,000
(compare KORE1's $110,000–$130,000 entry cloud engineer against
RockstarDeveloperUniversity's $82,000–$101,000). Presenting them with the same
confidence as BLS data would be misleading, so the chart marks them and the
detail panel spells out the caveat.

## 4. Interview questions

Every question in Module 2 is either candidate-reported or published in a hiring
guide. **None were invented.** The source appears under each question in the UI.

| Source type | Publishers used |
|---|---|
| Candidate-reported interviews | Glassdoor (Google, Discover, Indeed, Apple, Netflix, Microsoft reports) |
| Employer / careers guides | Indeed Career Guide (US, UK, Canada, India, Australia, Singapore), Indeed Hiring Guide |
| Practitioner guides | Tech Interview Handbook, Salesforce Ben, InterviewPrep, Infosec, Resume Worded, Springboard, Eduyush, BrainStation, IGotAnOffer, Exponent, InterviewQuery, Poised, Free-Work.com |

**A caveat worth stating on camera:** Glassdoor reports are self-selected — people
who had a memorable interview are more likely to post about it. So the bank
reflects what candidates *remember being asked*, which skews toward the
distinctive and the difficult. That is arguably the right skew for a practice
tool, but it is a bias, not a neutral sample.

## 6. AI disclosure

Per the case: *"You are explicitly encouraged to use AI tools to help you write
code, research career paths, and draft content. This is not cheating; it is the
point."*

**What AI was used for:**

- Locating and cross-checking sources for salary and outlook data.
- Drafting the model ("strong version") answers in Module 2.
- Writing the application code, the design tokens, and the feedback engine.
- Writing these docs.

**What was verified by hand:**

- Every salary figure was checked against the linked source and carries a
  `payBasis` flag recording how reliable that source is.
- Every interview question was traced to a named publisher.
- The feedback engine was tested against all 72 model answers, which surfaced
  three real bugs in AI-written code that looked correct on the page. See
  `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §5.

**What is explicitly labelled as not real:** the model answers describe invented
projects. The UI says so directly under each one — *"Do not memorise it — the
details are invented. Replace them with things that actually happened to you,
keeping the shape."* Putting a fabricated anecdote into a real interview is worse
than a weak honest answer.
